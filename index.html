What the atlas has found

Two findings — and two checks that the method holds

Independent, public-data research: every number traces to an open source, and the method is built to be broken. Two substantive findings — and, kept separate, two checks that the tooling behind them holds up:

The big idea

The chokepoint is rarely the mine

Of 58 supply chains, only 5 are constrained by geology. The other 53 were built by capital and policy — so they can be rebuilt.

The Chokepoint Map → The flagship

The refiner is not the source

For 17 of 32 materials the top exporter never mined it — and the gap holds in tonnes, not just dollars. Not a unit artifact.

The origin gap → Method check

The forecast is tested, and honest about it

Out-of-sample, last year names this year's top exporter 84% of the time — but that's the naive benchmark, not proof of skill, and we say where it fails (turning points).

The nowcast backtest → Method check

Public data recovers the IEA’s risk list

The atlas's public-data risk index independently reproduces 4 of the IEA's 6 discrete high-risk minerals in its top third.

How we know

Public data, reconciled, and falsifiable

No paid databases, no black boxes. Every number traces to an open source, the reconciliation is validated against the official series, and the whole thing is built to be broken — through three rounds of adversarial review that caught and published three real errors.
