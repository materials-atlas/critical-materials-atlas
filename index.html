Who mines critical raw materials, who refines them and who sells them

The atlas follows each material from reserves to mine, refinery and export market. It uses public statistics only: the US Geological Survey, the British Geological Survey, World Mining Data, the International Energy Agency and UN customs records as reconciled by CEPII. Trade is covered from 2002 to 2024.

In 2024 Switzerland was the largest exporter of gold and mined none of it.

It is not an exception. For most materials measured, the country that exports the most is not the country that mines the most. Often the seller is another mining country. In the cases below it is a refinery, a smelter or a trading hub in a country that mines almost none of the material.

Findings

  1. The largest exporter is usually not the largest miner. The result holds whether trade is counted in dollars or in tonnes. The origin gap
  2. In most value chains the most concentrated stage comes after the mine. It is usually refining, by-product recovery or product qualification. In a few chains it is the mine itself. The chokepoint map

Checks on the method

  1. The nowcast is tested against a naive benchmark. Out of sample, last year’s largest exporter is still the largest exporter 84% of the time. None of the forecasting models tested beats that benchmark reliably, and the page says so. Nowcast backtest
  2. The risk index is checked against the IEA. Built from public data alone, it places most of the minerals the IEA rates as high-risk in its own top third. IEA cross-check

Tools

  • Alternatives to a concentrated supplier. For each chokepoint, which countries and companies could supply an alternative, and which are building one.
  • Reallocation stress test. Removes the largest exporter of a material and reallocates the remaining exports, with a map before and after.
  • Importer exposure. For each importing country and material, the share of supply that comes from a single source.

Method and data

Every figure traces to a public source. The trade reconciliation is validated against the official CEPII series, each commodity carries a reliability grade, and errors found in review are published with their corrections. The code and the data are open.
