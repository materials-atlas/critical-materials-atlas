Materials and trade in defence supply chains

Production shares and trade records show where materials come from. Antimony imports show how origins and unit values changed around export controls.

Evidence review · 25 November 2026 · Claim register

This note asks what production shares, import origins and historical export controls can establish about selected materials used across civilian and defence industries. It examines five material groups that illustrate mining, refining or component production with documented controls. The selection serves as a set of examples. It gives no ranking of all industrial inputs.

Finding. China-origin imports of selected antimony products fell steeply in the EU series after the 2024 controls. The EU total for the same basket held up while customs unit values rose. The separate US series shows a fall in total imports. These changes describe aggregate markets. The records give no purchase volumes or output effects for individual defence programmes. Antimony mine shares depend on the source edition

The Atlas BGS series puts China at 43% in 2024. The 48% figure in the pilot matches the USGS estimate for 2023 in its 2024 edition. Its 2025 edition estimates 60% for 2024. The 2026 edition revises the estimates for China and several other producers. It puts China at 33.6% in 2024 and 36.4% in 2025. The published world totals exclude US mine output. These estimates have different reporting coverage and revision dates. The mine series alone gives no trend in refining. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2024, Antimony. Trade exposure has a different denominator

EU magnet imports. For CN 85051110 in 2024, China accounted for 91.1% by mass (18,246,441 of 20,028,179 kg) and 92.7% by value of extra-EU imports. The code covers metal permanent magnets and magnet blanks containing specified rare earths, including neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium or samarium. Its scope differs from the IEA’s sintered magnet production series. It groups several chemistries and serves many end uses. Eurostat Comext DS-045409. Atlas magnet study.

US antimony market. USGS estimates net import reliance at 91% of apparent consumption in 2025. The numerator is imports minus exports of oxide and unwrought metal or powder. This measures the market’s reliance on imports from all sources. It gives no defence procurement share. The public USGS stockpile table lists potential transactions. Estimating coverage would require data on usable inventories and the rate of consumption. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, Antimony.

The EU series uses Eurostat extra-EU imports and excludes UK and Northern Ireland partner codes, following the filed Atlas analysis. Its basket covers ore and concentrates plus unwrought material, scrap and articles. The US Census series covers general imports of unwrought material, scrap and articles. Oxide is outside both baskets. Unit values are declared value divided by reported weight. They change with prices, product mix and reporting. Filed analysis and customs definitions. Download the monthly series (CSV).

The two windows are descriptive. EU imports from China and total US imports were already falling before the announcement. The December US-specific measures overlap the later window. The records support a fall in direct imports from China and different changes in the two observed market totals. They leave demand, stocks, the causes of the changes and military end use unresolved.

Price reports. USGS lists annual antimony metal benchmark averages of USD 10.24 and USD 25 per pound for 2024 and 2025, respectively, in its February 2026 edition. The November outlook gives approximate annual averages of USD 10 and USD 24 and an end-April 2026 spot observation of about USD 12 per pound. The annual figures come from different publications and the spot quote is a point in time. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, Antimony. USGS World Minerals Outlook, November 2026, Antimony.

What the evidence can support

Global shares show where production occurs. Import origins show where an importing market buys a customs category. Recovery is a separate question about usable supply, inventories and time. Public procurement data have limited coverage of material and lower-tier supplier origins, as documented by the US Government Accountability Office. The evidence supports the market findings above. Programme-specific exposure, stock coverage and production losses require separate evidence. US GAO report on industrial supplier visibility, July 2025.
