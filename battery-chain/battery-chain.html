Battery manufacturing

When can battery factories replace disrupted inputs?

Battery ingredients change with the chemistry. Usable stocks, approved suppliers and access to upstream materials determine how long a factory can keep producing.

Evidence release · 24 November 2026

Production geography European exposure Disruption model Trade records Sources

Battery recipes shape supply exposure

China produces most battery cells and an even larger share of the active materials used in electric car batteries. Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) avoids nickel and cobalt in the cathode. Both designs studied here use graphite anodes, and the LFP cathode uses a different set of refined inputs. A factory can weather a lost shipment if it has compatible stock and replacement material arrives in time. The model below tests these conditions for two hypothetical EU production lines.

Lithium, nickel, cobalt, phosphate and natural graphite have different supply maps. Synthetic graphite follows a different feedstock route.

Nickel manganese cobalt (NMC) cathodes need battery-grade nickel and cobalt. LFP cathodes use lithium, iron and phosphate. Battery-grade purified phosphoric acid and coated graphite can have concentrated supply even when phosphate rock or carbon feedstocks are widely available. The IEA reported LFP at below 10% of the global electric-car battery market in 2020, just under 30% in 2022 and nearly half in 2024. These are rounded benchmarks from different editions. A European cell plant can rely on imported materials

The European Union accounted for almost 15% of global EV battery deployment in 2025. LFP supplied more than 10% of EU EV battery demand, and the IEA finds that almost all of that LFP came from China. Roughly 30% arrived as batteries and nearly 70% inside imported EVs. These shares track EU demand and direct or embedded imports of finished batteries. What existing studies already measure

The IEA tested how much lithium-ion battery deployment outside China could be met by manufacturing capacity located outside China, by stage and chemistry, at 85% nameplate utilisation. Its 2025 denominator covers electric vehicles and battery energy storage, and its nickel-based group includes NMC and nickel cobalt aluminium. Published research has also traced indirect exposure through upstream inputs, bounded uncertainty with network flows, and evaluated how chemistry shifts and recycling affect prices after disruptions. The question tested here is narrower. Given fixed recipes and assigned input contracts, how do usable stocks and supplier approval change the monthly production loss? The cases illustrate outcomes under stated assumptions. IEA, Figure 7.15, 2024 network-flow study, 2024 policy study and 2026 disruption study.

The input that limits production can change over time

Modelled The example runs for 8 months and starts with a hypothetical NMC811 line and an LFP line, each rated at 1 GWh per month. For both, pre-disruption shipments meet full needs. A disruption removes specified deliveries from China and, in the traced case, the portion of other deliveries that requires disrupted Chinese upstream supply. A delivery already counted as direct exposure is counted once. Stocks start ready for use, and qualified replacement begins only after the stated delay. Production uses the current month's available materials and carries unused stocks into the next month.

The NMC811 line loses 20% of its baseline in the first month because its graphite stock runs low, while the LFP line loses 0%. Across the full period, NMC811 loses 23.25% and LFP loses 38%. Once the LFP inventory runs down, its cathode becomes the continuing constraint. In this example, the shorter and longer comparisons rank the lines differently.

What it takes to estimate a real disruption

For a plant-specific result, each material needs a supplier and upstream origin, usable inventory, dated commitments, compatible specifications and a firm qualification schedule. Equipment, power and the other cell inputs are assumed available here. The model holds the chemistry and cell rating fixed, allocates stocks and contracts separately to the lines, and leaves prices and downstream demand outside the calculation. The reviewed public evidence provides no validated EU-wide schedule of qualified alternatives, orders and inventories. The reported thresholds apply to the specified lines and sourcing assumptions.
