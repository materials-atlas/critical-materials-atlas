Aluminium · Research note

Aluminium production is concentrated in China, and bauxite trade in Guinea

China made 60% of the world's alumina and primary aluminium in 2024 and exported little of either. Guinea supplied 67% of traded bauxite, and nine-tenths of it went to China.

Evidence review · 25 November 2026 · Claim register

Aluminium is made in three steps. Bauxite is mined, refined into alumina (aluminium oxide), and the alumina is smelted into metal by electrolysis. This note sets out, for each step, where production takes place and how much of it crosses a border. It then traces where smelting countries get their alumina, checks whether the published tonnages fit together, and reports what the public sources say about electricity, unused capacity and recycling.

Finding. In 2024 China made 60% of the world's alumina and 60% of its primary aluminium, and mined 19% of its bauxite. It supplied 5% of the alumina and 1% of the unwrought aluminium that were traded, and took 87% of bauxite imports. Guinea mined 33% of the world's bauxite and shipped 67% of the bauxite that was traded, 90% of it to China. The concentration in metal is a concentration of production inside China. The concentration in trade is at the ore stage, between one exporter and one importer. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition. CEPII BACI V202601.

The two measures point in opposite directions along the chain. Bauxite production is the least concentrated of the three stages and bauxite trade the most: 43% of the tonnage mined crossed a border, two exporters (Guinea 67%, Australia 23%) supplied nine-tenths of it, and one importer took 87%. Alumina and aluminium production are highly concentrated, and their trade is not. 26% of alumina was traded, mostly from Australia (38%), Brazil (20%) and Indonesia (11%). The largest exporter of unwrought aluminium, Canada, had 10% of the market. CEPII BACI V202601.

The share of production that is traded is approximate. Customs weight can include moisture and USGS reports dry tonnes. Unwrought aluminium in trade includes alloys and remelted metal, and production is primary metal only.

Two-thirds of China's bauxite is imported, most of it from Guinea

China mined 80.5 million tonnes of bauxite in 2024 and imported 159.4 million tonnes. Imports were therefore about 66% of the ore available to its refineries. Guinea alone shipped 110.1 million tonnes, 69% of China's imports and about 46% of its total supply. The dependence runs both ways: 90% of Guinea's exports went to China. Cross-checks. The trade database gives China's 2024 bauxite imports as 159.4 million tonnes, of which 110.1 million from Guinea. Shanghai Metals Market, a trade publication, gives 158.8 and 110.2 million. The two agree. Guinea's own figures do not match as closely. Its statistics institute reports bauxite exports of 138.3 million tonnes in 2024, against 122.5 million in the trade database, and gives China's share as 86.7%, apparently by value. The gap changes sign: the institute's figure is 23 million tonnes below the trade database for 2022 and 13 million below for 2023, and 16 million above for 2024. A single cause such as moisture would not do that, and this note does not resolve it. USGS puts Guinea's 2024 output at 141.7 million dry tonnes, or 155 million tonnes on a wet basis. Indonesia stopped exporting ore, and its alumina exports have grown

Indonesia banned bauxite exports from June 2023. USGS reports that its exports fell from about 18 million tonnes in 2022 to about 2 million in 2023, all to China. Its latest yearbook gives output of 30.4 million tonnes in 2022, 9.9 million in 2023 and an estimated 9.9 million in 2024, all of the 2024 output consumed at home. The trade database shows the same break, and no Indonesian bauxite exports in 2024. Indonesia's alumina exports were already growing before the ban and reached 4.2 million tonnes in 2024, 11% of world alumina trade. That is more than three times the 1.2 million tonnes that USGS estimates Indonesia produced in 2024. The two figures cannot both be right, and the USGS one is an estimate that has not changed since 2022. USGS reports a new one-million-tonne refinery at Mempawah shipping its first alumina in April 2025 and a 500,000-tonne smelter in North Kalimantan due to start by the end of 2025. USGS 2023 Minerals Yearbook, Indonesia. USGS 2024 Minerals Yearbook, Indonesia. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition.

Of the eleven countries other than China, eight import more than half of their alumina: Russia, Canada, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Norway, Malaysia, Iceland and the United States. Together they made 15.3 million tonnes of metal in 2024, 53% of primary output outside China. USGS lists no alumina output for Bahrain, Norway, Malaysia and Iceland. The alumina comes from few places. Australia shipped 38% of traded alumina and Brazil 20%. One country supplied at least 70% of imports for Canada (Brazil 71%), the United Arab Emirates (Australia 81%), Bahrain (Australia 97%), Norway (Brazil 79%) and the United States (Brazil 77%). The pattern continues beyond the twelve: Australia supplied at least 99% of the alumina imported by New Zealand, Mozambique and South Africa, 83% of that imported by Qatar, 72% of that imported by Argentina and 52% of that imported by Oman. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. CEPII BACI V202601.

The customs record of the metal does not show this link. Metal bought from Bahrain depends on alumina from Australia, and metal bought from Norway on alumina from Brazil. The same holds one step further back. The United Arab Emirates refines part of its alumina from imported bauxite. USGS reported that a new mine in Guinea began shipping to that refinery in 2019 and would be its principal source of bauxite. It also reported the 2025 revocation of a United Arab Emirates producer's mining licences in Guinea, described above. The USGS text does not say whether those were the licences of that mine. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2020 edition. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition.

Suppliers changed between 2019 and 2024. Russia's two largest suppliers in 2019 were Ukraine (35%) and Australia (26%). In 2024 neither was among its five largest, which were led by China (36%) and Indonesia (20%). The largest supplier of India and of Malaysia was Australia in 2019 and Indonesia in 2024. This note records the change and does not examine its causes. CEPII BACI V202601.

Where the check does not fit. Three rows are more than a tenth away from the survey ratio: Brazil, Iceland and the United States. The United States row gives 2.5 tonnes of alumina per tonne of metal. USGS says that in 2025 about 70% of the alumina produced in the United States went to smelters and the rest to other products, so part of the alumina in that row was not used for metal. USGS also reports higher US alumina imports for 2024, 1.34 million tonnes, than the 1.10 million in the trade database. The customs code covers alumina that is not smelter grade as well. The rows for Brazil and Iceland are not explained here. Two rows fail in the other direction: Bahrain (1.85) and Malaysia (1.80) are below 1.89, the least alumina that can contain a tonne of aluminium, so those smelters drew on stocks or the trade record is short. One bauxite record is incomplete as well. The trade database has 0.4 million tonnes of bauxite imports for the United Arab Emirates in 2024 and none from Guinea, against 10.7 million tonnes from Guinea in 2023 and 3.8 million in 2022. The country made 2.5 million tonnes of alumina in 2024, which at the USGS rule of two tonnes of bauxite per tonne of alumina needs 5.1 million tonnes. The 2023 figure is about twice that, so the record for this route looks unreliable in both years. A missing record would also account for part of the gap between Guinea's own export figure and the trade database, noted above.

A recorded interruption: Brazil, 2018

One partial interruption of alumina supply is on record. In February 2018 the Government of Brazil ordered a 6.3-million-tonne-a-year alumina refinery, and the bauxite mine near it, to cut to half of capacity. A court lifted the restriction in May 2019. USGS puts Brazil's alumina output at 10.9 million tonnes in 2017 and 8.3 million in 2018. The fall was about 2% of world output. USGS reports that the refinery was back at full capacity by the end of 2019. Brazil's alumina exports fell from 8.7 million tonnes in 2017 to 6.8 million in 2018, or by 22%, and were 8.2 million in 2020. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2019 edition. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2020 edition. USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022, bauxite and alumina. CEPII BACI V202601.

Norway took most of its alumina from Brazil. It imported 2.42 million tonnes in 2017 and 2.41 million in 2018. Tonnage from Brazil fell by 0.40 million and tonnage from other countries rose by 0.40 million. USGS gives Norway's metal output as 1.23 million tonnes in 2017 and 1.29 million in 2018. Brazil's own metal output went from 0.80 million tonnes to 0.66 million, a fall of 18%. Canada's alumina imports from Brazil fell by 0.64 million tonnes and its total alumina imports by 0.41 million, and its metal output went from 3.21 to 2.92 million tonnes. This note has not established whether those two changes are connected. USGS, aluminum, 2019 edition. USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022, aluminum.

On these figures Norway's smelters replaced the Brazilian tonnage from other suppliers within the year, while metal output in Brazil itself fell. The case is a small one: half of one refinery, about 2% of world alumina output, from February 2018 to May 2019. It does not show what the replacement cost, since prices are not examined here, and it says little about a larger loss.

The same study measured government support to 17 large producers at between USD 20 and 70 billion over 2013 to 2017. Both power costs and public support bear on where smelters are built. This note does not estimate how much of China's share either one explains. OECD (2019), The aluminium value chain.

The moisture figure is of the same order as the gap of 8.6% between the dry and wet tonnages that USGS gives for Guinea, quoted above. The USGS wet tonnage is an estimate and the ministry's average is a simple mean across companies. Apart from the Guinean table, these are reserve grades and not averages of what was shipped. The two bases cannot be compared directly, and no figure was found for Chinese domestic ore or for Indonesia. The world mined 3.0 tonnes of bauxite per tonne of alumina made in 2024, and grades this far below pure alumina are consistent with that. They also mean that a country's share of bauxite tonnage is not its share of contained aluminium. Indian Minerals Yearbook 2022, Bauxite. Guinea, Ministry of Mines and Geology, statistics bulletin no. 27.

Recycling

The industry association's material-flow model puts recycled metal at 36% of world aluminium production in 2024. This is model output and not a survey. In the United States, metal recovered from old scrap was equal to 28% of apparent consumption in 2025. USGS changed that calculation in its 2026 edition to leave out exported scrap, so the figure cannot be compared with earlier editions. The OECD study cites an industry estimate that recycling saves as much as 95% of the energy used in smelting. International Aluminium Institute, Global Aluminium Cycle. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition. OECD (2019), The aluminium value chain.

How firm the production figures are

USGS revised its 2024 bauxite figures heavily between editions. Indonesia went from an estimated 32 million tonnes to 9.9 million, China from 93 to 80.5 million, Guinea from 130 to 142 million, and the world total from 450 to 428 million. Guinea's share of 2024 output is 29% on the earlier figures and 33% on the later ones. China's share of 2024 primary aluminium is 60.4% in the USGS table and 59.4% in the industry association's series, which gives 59.7% for 2025. The association's figure for China is its own estimate from analysts' data. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2025 edition. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. International Aluminium Institute, primary aluminium production.

What the customs records cover

The trade table below uses four customs codes and shows values. Bauxite of every grade is one code. Alumina of every grade is one code, so smelter-grade and chemical-grade alumina cannot be separated, and aluminium hydroxide is a different code that is not included. Unwrought aluminium is split into unalloyed metal of at least 99% purity and alloys. Neither is the same as primary metal: remelted metal can be unalloyed and primary metal can be sold as alloy. What remains open

Production shares and trade shares are measured. Four things are not, or only in part. The first is the aluminium content of bauxite as shipped, by origin and on one basis: the reserve grades above are a partial answer. The second is stocks: the figures above for China come from trade surveys with unstated coverage, and no world figure exists. The third is how much of the location of smelting is explained by power costs and how much by public support. The fourth is the carbon intensity of the metal by power source. The previous version of this page gave figures for that, and they have not been checked against a source in this review. Two further things were looked for and not found: a complete list of idle plants with their condition, and the time and cost of qualifying a different alumina or bauxite at a refinery or smelter.
