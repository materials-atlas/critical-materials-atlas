Aluminium · Research note

Aluminium production is concentrated in China, and bauxite trade in Guinea

China made 60% of the world's alumina and primary aluminium in 2024 and exported little of either. Guinea supplied 67% of traded bauxite, and nine-tenths of it went to China.

Evidence review · 25 November 2026 · Claim register

Aluminium is made in three steps. Bauxite is mined, refined into alumina (aluminium oxide), and the alumina is smelted into metal by electrolysis. This note sets out, for each step, where production takes place and how much of it crosses a border. It then checks whether the published tonnages fit together, and what the public sources say about electricity and recycling.

Finding. In 2024 China made 60% of the world's alumina and 60% of its primary aluminium, and mined 19% of its bauxite. It supplied 5% of the alumina and 1% of the unwrought aluminium that were traded, and took 87% of bauxite imports. Guinea mined 33% of the world's bauxite and shipped 67% of the bauxite that was traded, 90% of it to China. The concentration in metal is a concentration of production inside China. The concentration in trade is at the ore stage, between one exporter and one importer. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition. CEPII BACI V202601.

The two measures point in opposite directions along the chain. Bauxite production is the least concentrated of the three stages and bauxite trade the most: 43% of the tonnage mined crossed a border, two exporters (Guinea 67%, Australia 23%) supplied nine-tenths of it, and one importer took 87%. Alumina and aluminium production are highly concentrated, and their trade is not. 26% of alumina was traded, mostly from Australia (38%), Brazil (20%) and Indonesia (11%). The largest exporter of unwrought aluminium, Canada, had 10% of the market. CEPII BACI V202601.

The share of production that is traded is approximate. Customs weight can include moisture and USGS reports dry tonnes. Unwrought aluminium in trade includes alloys and remelted metal, and production is primary metal only.

China exported 2% of the alumina it made and 0.6% of its primary metal in unwrought form, and was the second-largest importer of unwrought aluminium (11% of world imports). Its aluminium reaches other countries mainly after further processing. China exported 5.8 million tonnes of semi-finished aluminium in 2024, 29% of world exports in those categories, a tonnage equal to 13% of its primary output. Two-thirds of China's bauxite is imported, most of it from Guinea

China mined 80.5 million tonnes of bauxite in 2024 and imported 159.4 million tonnes. Imports were therefore about 66% of the ore available to its refineries. Guinea alone shipped 110.1 million tonnes, 69% of China's imports and about 46% of its total supply. The dependence runs both ways: 90% of Guinea's exports went to China. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. CEPII BACI V202601.

Guinea's exports rose from 11.5 million tonnes in 2005 to 122.5 million in 2024. China's share of world bauxite imports rose from 6% to 87% over the same years. USGS estimates Guinea's 2025 output at 150 million tonnes, 34% of the world total. It also reports that in August 2025 the Guinean government revoked the mining licences of a subsidiary of a United Arab Emirates aluminium producer, after a dispute over building an alumina refinery, and gave the concessions to a state-backed company. Cross-checks. The trade database gives China's 2024 bauxite imports as 159.4 million tonnes, of which 110.1 million from Guinea. Shanghai Metals Market, a trade publication, gives 158.8 and 110.2 million. The two agree. Guinea's own figures do not match as closely. Its statistics institute reports bauxite exports of 138.3 million tonnes in 2024, against 122.5 million in the trade database, and gives China's share as 86.7%, apparently by value. The gap changes sign: the institute's figure is 23 million tonnes below the trade database for 2022 and 13 million below for 2023, and 16 million above for 2024. A single cause such as moisture would not do that, and this note does not resolve it. USGS puts Guinea's 2024 output at 141.7 million dry tonnes, or 155 million tonnes on a wet basis. Shanghai Metals Market, 20 January 2025. Guinea, national statistics institute, external trade 2024. USGS 2024 Minerals Yearbook, Guinea.

Indonesia stopped exporting ore, and its alumina exports have grown

Indonesia banned bauxite exports from June 2023. USGS reports that its exports fell from about 18 million tonnes in 2022 to about 2 million in 2023, all to China. Its latest yearbook gives output of 30.4 million tonnes in 2022, 9.9 million in 2023 and an estimated 9.9 million in 2024, all of the 2024 output consumed at home. The trade database shows the same break, and no Indonesian bauxite exports in 2024. Indonesia's alumina exports were already growing before the ban and reached 4.2 million tonnes in 2024, 11% of world alumina trade. That is more than three times the 1.2 million tonnes that USGS estimates Indonesia produced in 2024. The two figures cannot both be right, and the USGS one is an estimate that has not changed since 2022. USGS reports a new one-million-tonne refinery at Mempawah shipping its first alumina in April 2025 and a 500,000-tonne smelter in North Kalimantan due to start by the end of 2025. USGS 2023 Minerals Yearbook, Indonesia. USGS 2024 Minerals Yearbook, Indonesia. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition.

The alumina and metal figures fit. The world and Chinese ratios are within a few percent of the survey value, so China's refining is about the size its smelters need. The bauxite figures do not fit the simplest rule. World bauxite output of 428 million tonnes is 51% more than the 284 million tonnes that the USGS rule of two tonnes per tonne of alumina would require. The implied ratio of 3.0 is inside the range the OECD gives. Several things can raise it: ore of lower grade, moisture in the reported tonnage, bauxite used for purposes other than metal, and changes in stocks. This note does not measure any of them. One consequence is that a share of bauxite tonnage is not a share of the aluminium the ore contains. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. Coal supplied 50% of the smelting electricity in the reported world total and 69% in China. Hydropower supplied 31% of the reported total. China accounted for 60% of the reported electricity and 83% of the reported coal-fired electricity. The output not covered is outside China, so China's shares of the true world totals are a little lower. International Aluminium Institute, smelting power consumption.

The OECD's 2019 study of the industry says energy can be up to 40% of the cost of primary aluminium, depending on local power prices. The same study measured government support to 17 large producers at between USD 20 and 70 billion over 2013 to 2017. It found that the five largest recipients received 85% of it, most of that at the smelting stage, and that support was especially large in China and the Gulf Cooperation Council countries. Both power costs and public support bear on where smelters are built. This note does not estimate how much of China's share either one explains. OECD (2019), The aluminium value chain.

Chinese smelters produced at 99% of their year-end capacity in 2024. The world figure was 92% and the United States figure 52%. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition.

Recycling

The industry association's material-flow model puts recycled metal at 36% of world aluminium production in 2024. This is model output and not a survey. In the United States, metal recovered from old scrap was equal to 28% of apparent consumption in 2025. USGS changed that calculation in its 2026 edition to leave out exported scrap, so the figure cannot be compared with earlier editions. The OECD study cites an industry estimate that recycling saves as much as 95% of the energy used in smelting. International Aluminium Institute, Global Aluminium Cycle. USGS, aluminum, 2026 edition. OECD (2019), The aluminium value chain.

How firm the production figures are

USGS revised its 2024 bauxite figures heavily between editions. Indonesia went from an estimated 32 million tonnes to 9.9 million, China from 93 to 80.5 million, Guinea from 130 to 142 million, and the world total from 450 to 428 million. Guinea's share of 2024 output is 29% on the earlier figures and 33% on the later ones. China's share of 2024 primary aluminium is 60.4% in the USGS table and 59.4% in the industry association's series, which gives 59.7% for 2025. The association's figure for China is its own estimate from analysts' data. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2025 edition. USGS, bauxite and alumina, 2026 edition. International Aluminium Institute, primary aluminium production.

What the customs records cover

The trade table below uses four customs codes and shows values. Bauxite of every grade is one code. Alumina of every grade is one code, so smelter-grade and chemical-grade alumina cannot be separated, and aluminium hydroxide is a different code that is not included. Unwrought aluminium is split into unalloyed metal of at least 99% purity and alloys. Neither is the same as primary metal: remelted metal can be unalloyed and primary metal can be sold as alloy. Bauxite from Australia to China is 34.4 million tonnes in 2022, 14.3 million in 2023 and 40.3 million in 2024, at USD 30, 90 and 46 per tonne. The 2023 value is in line with the other years and the weight is not. It lowers the world total for 2023 and raises Guinea's share to 79%. This page does not use 2023 tonnage shares for bauxite. The record itself is unchanged. CEPII BACI V202601.

What remains open

Production shares and trade shares are measured. Four things are not. The first is the aluminium content of bauxite by origin, which would turn ore tonnage into metal. The second is stocks of bauxite and alumina in China, which decide how long an interruption could be absorbed. The third is how much of the location of smelting is explained by power costs and how much by public support. The fourth is the carbon intensity of the metal by power source. The previous version of this page gave figures for that, and they have not been checked against a source in this review.

Revision note · 25 November 2026

The previous overview was headed “Aluminium is congealed electricity”. It gave China's share as rising from about 16% at the mine to about 59% at the smelter, with a chart showing 21%, 58% and 59%, and said smelting went where power was cheapest. It attributed an energy figure of 12 to 15 megawatt-hours per tonne to USGS, whose chapter has no energy figure, and gave coal's share of smelting power as about 57% worldwide and close to 90% in China. This revision uses the published tables for each stage, adds trade by weight, corrects the power figures from the industry association's series, and says what is not established about cause. The claim register lists each change.
