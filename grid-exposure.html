Which parts of Europe's grid come from China, on a supply basis?

In 2024 China supplied 34% of the EU's transformer imports and 9% of its transformer supply. The difference between the two measures is the part of supply that the EU imports at all.

Working paper · 25 November 2026 · Presents a study filed on 21 September 2026

Summary

A country's share of another economy's imports is often read as a measure of dependence. It leaves out what the importing economy makes for itself. This paper measures China's part in seven kinds of electricity-grid equipment used in the European Union in two ways: as a share of imports from outside the EU, and as a share of apparent supply, which adds EU production and subtracts EU exports.

Finding. In 2024 China supplied 34.2% of the EU's imports of liquid-dielectric transformers and 9.1% of their apparent supply. For grain-oriented electrical steel, the core material of transformers, the two figures were 51.9% and 24.2%. In six of the seven components the supply share was less than half the import share. The exception was large inverters, at 82.3% of imports and 73.0% of supply, and that figure is the least reliable of the seven. The supply share equals the import share multiplied by the part of supply that is imported, so the gap between the two measures is set by how much the EU makes for itself. Eurostat Prodcom and Comext.

The measure describes direct exposure to one supplying country. It says nothing about whether equipment is scarce, which is the subject of the companion paper on transformer procurement.

The measure

Apparent supply is EU sold production plus imports from outside the EU minus exports to outside the EU, by value. China's share of apparent supply divides gross imports from China by that total. The United Kingdom is excluded from the trade sums in every year. The study design, the seven components and their product codes were filed on 21 September 2026, before any value for the five new components was read. Pre-registration.

China's share of supply = China's share of imports × imports as a share of apparent supply

The second term is import penetration. It is the reason the two shares differ. Where the EU imports little of what it uses, even a supplier with most of the import market has a small part of supply. Where the EU imports nearly all of what it uses, the two shares are close.

The filed design read each result through the ratio of the supply share to the import share: below 0.5, the import share overstates dependence and the supply share is the number to use; from 0.5 to 0.8, it partly overstates it; at 0.8 or above, it is a fair guide. Added after filing That ratio is import penetration. The filed text did not name it, and naming it adds no assumption.

The EU was a net exporter of transformers, high-voltage cable, switchgear, switchboards and large inverters in 2024, and a net importer of electrical steel and meters. The ratio ranged from 0.15 for insulated conductors above 1,000 V to 0.89 for large inverters. On the filed rule the import share overstates dependence for six components, by a factor of two to seven. Electrical steel, at 0.47, is the borderline case. For large inverters the import share is a fair guide.

The filed bands name the size of the supply share: low (under 10%), material (10% to 25%), high (25% to 50%) and critical (above 50%). The filing says of them: “The bands describe; they do not measure strategic risk, which depends on what could replace the supply.” “Critical” there means a share above half. It does not mean a shortage. Filed study, section 4.6c. Pre-registration.

The official 2024 correspondence confirms the transformer and electrical-steel code links. The inverter link is partial: the trade code also feeds a production line for telecom and computer power supplies. The transformer lines run from units of 650 kVA or less to units above 10 MVA. The highest bin still includes equipment far below a large power transformer, which the US Department of Energy defines as 100 MVA or more. The electrical-steel lines split at 600 mm of width and give no grade or end use. The inverter line starts at 7.5 kVA and covers solar, storage and industrial equipment as well as grid converters. The meter line includes household smart meters. Eurostat flags rounded production values with R and estimates with E. Prodcom List 2024.

By mass, the electrical-steel result is close to the result by value: 56.5% of imports and 26.3% of apparent supply in 2024, with some production tonnage rounded.

Modelled Illustrative ranges, added on 24 November 2026. Trade valuation scales all customs values equally. The scope check removes 10% of apparent supply and assigns it either to Chinese-origin supply or to other supply. Re-export varies the retained fraction of Chinese imports from 75% to 100%. The ranges describe sensitivity and carry no probability.

On the combined range, three components stay inside their band and four reach into a neighbouring one. The transformer share stays between 5.7% and 11.1% on the combined range. The inverter share does not hold up in the same way. Moving EU production by 10% either way gives 65.5% to 82.5%. Eurostat publishes the 2024 inverter production value rounded to a base of €3 billion. Applying its rounding rule to production alone allows a share as low as 34.0%, and at the low end of the rounding range the supply total could be zero or negative, so the formula gives no upper limit. The filed study reads the inverter row as a dependence in a broad class of large inverters, of imprecise size, that is not shown to be a grid one. Eurostat DS-059358 quick guide, p. 2.

From 2026 the EU customs classification splits inverters by function instead of size. In January to July 2026 China supplied 97% of EU imports of solar inverters and 57% of imports of other inverters, at any size. The split does not show how much of the large-inverter line is solar. Filed study, section 4.6c.

The United States, for comparison

US customs data for 2024 show a much smaller direct share for the same supplier: China accounted for 0.6% of the import value of grain-oriented electrical steel and 3.4% of the import value of liquid-dielectric transformers. Japan and South Korea supplied most US imports of the steel. No apparent-supply figure is formed for the United States here, because no production series on the same product definitions was used. A low share from one country can sit beside heavy reliance on imports from others. September trade study, section 4.7.

What the measure cannot say

Apparent supply is not consumption. Sold production leaves out what a manufacturer uses itself. Production is valued at the factory gate and imports include freight. Stock changes are not observed. Gross imports from China include goods that may later be re-exported, and public totals cannot trace them by origin. The production and trade codes are matched on product descriptions and, for inverters, only partly correspond.

The measure is direct. It counts a transformer imported from China and does not count Chinese electrical steel inside a transformer made in the EU or imported from a third country. The seven components are therefore never added together. On these figures China supplied 24.2% of the EU's electrical steel in 2024, so some exposure sits inside EU-made transformers that the 9.1% does not show.

The measure is about one supplier's part in supply. Whether equipment can be obtained on time depends on factory capacity, order books and qualified inputs. A component with a small share from one country can be in short supply worldwide, and a component with a large share can be readily available.

Relation to the filed study

The design was filed on 21 September 2026 and its results were published as sections 4.6b and 4.6c of the September trade study, which remain as filed. This paper presents those results on their own, with three additions made after filing and marked as such: the naming of the filed ratio as import penetration, the product-correspondence check against the official 2024 list, and the sensitivity ranges. No filed figure has been changed. The independent second extraction reproduces each published 2024 share to the first decimal place. Filed study, sections 4.6b and 4.6c. Pre-registration.
