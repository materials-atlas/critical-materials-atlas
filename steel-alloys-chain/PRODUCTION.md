## Findings of this review

- A world share is not an importer's exposure. Ferronickel: Indonesia 93% of trade, 97% of China's imports, none of
  the United States'. Ferroniobium is the one alloy where all six markets had the same largest supplier in 2024; in 2019
  and 2022 India's largest recorded supplier was Singapore, a re-exporter.
- Mine shares and alloy shares differ for chromium, vanadium, nickel and manganese.
- The previous page gave a US figure as a world one (niobium 77%). Other USGS shares on this subject are US figures as
  well (vanadium "more than 90%", nickel "more than 85%", the recycling shares) and are labelled as such.
- USGS revises its estimates: China's 2024 vanadium output went from 70,000 to 84,000 t between editions.

## Limits of the trade figures

- World trade counts re-exports twice. The Netherlands, China, Malaysia and Singapore appear with 16% of recorded
  ferroniobium exports.
- A market that is, or contains, the leading exporter is marked "own exporter" in the table: the EU for ferrovanadium
  (Austria), India for ferromanganese, South Korea for ferromolybdenum.
- HS 720260 is headed "Ferro-nickel". Chapter 72 note 1(c) makes an iron alloy with more than 10% of alloying elements a
  ferro-alloy, so nickel pig iron above that grade is recorded under it. The page says this and no longer says simply
  that the code includes nickel pig iron.
- The notes inside the frozen value extract (`out/steel_trade.json`) are unchanged and include wording this review
  withdrew ("processing China-led", "~92% traces to Brazilian niobium"). The page shows corrected coverage text.
- The OECD Steel Outlook 2026 was read from a copy hosted by a third party; the link given is the OECD's own.

A second reading of the whole page against the sources, same day, led to these changes before publication: the sentence
that four of the six metals are alloyed in a different pattern of countries was replaced by a statement for each metal;
the ferrovanadium sentence now names the three leading exporters; three table cells that read 0% are marked "own
exporter"; the re-export note was added; the niobium paragraph states its units and the third producer; contrary
chromium figures were added; and the coal sentence gives the Australian table's different ranking.

## Not found or not opened

- OECD, Annual market and policy report on steelmaking raw materials, 2025 edition: the document would not open. A
  figure quoted from it in an earlier review (China 51% of ferrochromium, 2024) is not used.
- A world figure for the share of vanadium, manganese or molybdenum used in steel.
- Company shares of niobium supply on one basis. The Brazilian mining agency publishes a royalty split, which is not
  a production share, and its editions disagree on the basis of the production figure.
- The full text of the steel standards. Composition limits are from producer data sheets that name the standard, and
  from the standard's own page for A572 (scope from the 2025 edition, alloy table from the 2012a edition).
- Stocks of ferroalloys.

## Not done

- No metal-content conversion of ferroalloy tonnage.
- No estimate of the effect of an interruption, and no substitution model.
- No expert review.
