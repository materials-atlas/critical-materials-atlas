# Aerospace claim register

Evidence review · 25 November 2026

This register audits the previously published, undated pilot. Its
[evidence record][old-evidence], [page shell][old-page] and [trade extract][old-trade]
are pinned to the reviewed public release. The live evidence and trade JSON
matched those checked-in files at review. Observation years and claims below
refer to that version unless otherwise specified.

| ID | Original wording or reported value | Measure and vintage | Status and disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A01 | “At every step downstream the supply gets narrower” | Interpretation across unlike stages and units | **Withdrawn.** The like-for-like titanium case gives a lower country HHI for aviation-grade capacity. Comparing HHI across country, firm and customs series does not establish a progression or a binding constraint. [JRC] |
| A02 | “~81 t world rhenium output” and “~37%” Chile | USGS 2025 estimate, tonnes of contained rhenium | **Qualified.** 30,000 / 81,000 kg is 37.0%. The USGS mine production table measures mine-linked recovery and excludes secondary production (note 7). Chile's figure includes recovery from domestic and imported residues (note 9). It is neither an ore-origin share nor a firm's share. The legacy ~55% in the library and production notes is not supported by this vintage. [USGS] |
| A03 | “0 mines” and “supply is set by copper economics, not rhenium demand” | Inference from primary rhenium recovery, USGS 2026 | **Narrowed.** The USGS table associates primary recovery with copper and molybdenum production. It provides no mine census or estimate of the response to a change in price. Recovery rates, recovered feedstock, scrap and availability matter. [USGS] |
| A04 | “~80% of rhenium goes to turbine-engine superalloys (~15% to petroleum catalysts)” | USGS estimated end uses in the Domestic Production and Use subsection | **Qualified.** The chapter gives this end-use split in its US discussion, without identifying the figures as a global market split. The revised study leaves out a global percentage. [USGS] |
| A05 | “China ~71% of tonnage” with Japan 17% and Russia 13% | Mixed 2024 and 2022 sponge output | **Replaced.** Use a single year and edition for comparisons. USGS 2026 estimates 260 kt in China and 370 kt worldwide in 2025, about 70%. It revises its 2024 estimate to 256 of about 380 kt, with the world total excluding withheld US domestic production for that year. [USGS] |
| A06 | “China makes ~71% of titanium sponge yet almost none is aerospace-qualified” | Sponge output mixed with qualification inference | **Replaced.** JRC Figure 5 gives China 63% of all-quality 2023 operating capacity and an estimated 12% of aviation-grade capacity. Japan leads the latter at 41%. The JRC uses a domestic-demand assumption for China. The estimates do not identify which buyers have approved each source. [JRC] |
| A07 | “~28% … is aerospace-qualified” (89 of about 320 kt) | 2024 mixed-source output proxy | **Withdrawn as a global share.** The market report sums total sponge production from Japan, Kazakhstan and Saudi Arabia and excludes China and Russia. This numerator is not a global census of approved output. The grade comparison uses the JRC's 414 and 157 kt per year of operating capacity in 2023. These totals and classifications describe a different year and measure. [Prior source][argus] · [JRC] |
| A08 | “Japan 80%”, “Saudi Arabia 13%”, “Kazakhstan 9%” of US aerospace sponge | Undated rounded import shares totaling 102% | **Replaced.** USGS 2026 gives 77%, 13%, 8% and 2% other for US sponge imports in 2021–24 across grades and uses. It reports zero US domestic sponge output in 2025 after the last operating plant closed in 2024. An idle plant cannot be counted as current supply. [USGS] |
| A09 | “~6 aerospace-qualified producers” and “~a decade to qualify a new source” | Facility-count and timing assumptions | **Unverified.** The record provides no consistent definition of qualified producer, buyer approval roster or dates by material grade or part. Boeing’s filing discusses a year or more for some alternative major raw material sources. It does not set a universal timetable. [JRC] · [Boeing] |
| A10 | “a low-yield … stage held by a handful of firms” | Illustrative company evidence without a series | **Unverified as a market estimate.** The record has no firm universe, yield series, qualified throughput or spare capacity. The current civil example concerns inspections and shop capacity, and cannot identify a casting bottleneck. [FAA directive] · [JetBlue] |
| A11 | “55 / 26 / 18”, “$81.0B” and a three-firm engine oligopoly | Secondary estimate without a common segment, unit and joint-venture attribution rule | **Removed.** Shares sum to 99%. Squaring gives 4,025 before treatment of missing shares. Neither this sum nor normalisation fixes the missing market definition or overlaps in counting joint ventures and parent groups. Use a defined civil segment and a dated shipment, fleet or other denominator, with each programme counted once. [Original source record][old-evidence] |
| A12 | “4,026 vs 1,345” for maker vs export concentration | Maker estimate vs 2024 HS 841112 country exporter HHI | **Partly reproducible.** The published export HHI is 0.1345 on a 0–1 scale. The extract stores eight leading exporters and does not allow a fresh all-exporter HHI calculation from its listed shares. A country export-value HHI is not a check on a manufacturer HHI. [Trade extract][old-trade] |
| A13 | “China's share of world titanium-sponge exports, 2002–2024” | BACI V202601 HS 810820 traded value | **Re-labelled.** Retain the data and describe unwrought titanium and powders, including ingot and sponge. The 2024 share is 6.1% of value, not tonnage or qualifying grades. The series spans HS02 and HS17. [Trade extract][old-trade] |
| A14 | US “3.1:1 import-dependent”, “nickel … imports only” and finished engines “0.4:1” | 2024 HS 810820, 750210 and 841112 annual value | **Partly reproduced.** For titanium, $483.3 million / $156.5 million = 3.09. For the turbojet heading, $8.64 billion / $20.12 billion = 0.43. These ratios measure a trade balance. The US does not appear among the extract's leading nickel exporters, which does not establish zero exports. [Trade extract][old-trade] |
| A15 | “Largest single node (share of stage supply)” and “The binding node is downstream” | Mixed country production, bilateral imports and firm estimates | **Withdrawn.** The pilot offered no flow, inventory or substitution observations sufficient to estimate rerouting or production losses. The illustrative model states its own assumptions. Stock depletion and partial replacement can cause different results with identical initial disruption size. |
| A16 | “Only ~0.4 kg of every 1.3 kg … ends up in the finished part” and “up to 90% is machined away” | Process mass balance and example loss rates | **Qualified and omitted.** 0.4 / 1.3 is 31%. This is a cited process example, with a different denominator from machining yield. The pilot also gives price, energy and technical readiness estimates for a steel substitution pathway. It does not show that it meets the same approval and service requirements. [Original source record][old-evidence] |
| A17 | “Superalloy remelting … 0%” and recovery routes “not yet technologically or economically feasible” | Generalisation from a literature review and test-specific yields | **Corrected.** USGS records commercial hydrometallurgical recovery of rhenium and production of superalloy meltstock from revert. Industry's 20–25 t secondary estimate for 2025 excludes catalysts recovered in a closed loop. The study does not extrapolate individual process yields or publish a combined total. [USGS] |
| A18 | “Rhenium … scores the LOWEST supply risk” and “the index underrates the real chokepoint” | EU 2023 criticality categories and scores | **Inference withdrawn.** The EU's economy-wide measure has a different scope from civil engine programme availability. Its reported scores do not demonstrate understatement absent a programme-level outcome and comparison. [Original source record][old-evidence] |
| A19 | “Qualified casting and engines are not” stockable and “the only lever … is to start now” | Hypothesis about mitigation | **Withdrawn.** The relevant question is what stock of approved material, components or spares can be accessed and at what cost. Inventory, production, shop availability and delay can be assessed separately with buyer records. [Boeing] · [JetBlue] |

## Added evidence and calculations

| ID | Result and class | Underlying evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A20 | **Calculated.** Country HHI of 4,396.48 for all-quality titanium sponge capacity and 3,014 for aviation grade, down 31.4%. The largest two shares fall from 79% to 71%. Aviation-grade capacity is 157 / 414 kt per year, 37.9%. | [JRC] 2023 operating capacity, Figure 5, with rounded values and the estimated all-quality residual. The 0.2-point residual is not a measured country share. Aviation grade is the report's estimate, not verified programme-qualified or spare capacity. |
| A21 | **Reported outcome.** Boeing suspended purchases from Russia in 2022 and reported continuity based on existing inventory and alternatives. | [Boeing], Raw Materials, Parts and Subassemblies. Company-level comparator. The filing gives no mass balance, timing by programme or causal estimate for the wider industry. |
| A22 | **Reported and projected.** JetBlue reported eight aircraft grounded for lack of engines on 30 June 2025. It expected about 300 days per shop visit for removed engines. | [JetBlue], Operational Update, and [FAA directive]. JetBlue attributed the shortage to required inspections and other reliability issues. The duration was a company expectation. The case cannot rank upstream suppliers or quantify usable shop capacity market-wide. |
| A23 | **Modelled.** Illustrative inventory and replacement sensitivity for a single civil buyer over twelve months. | Demand is constant, a supplier's deliveries are suspended for the horizon, and alternatives become available at a flat rate after a stated lag. Results are unmet units summed over months with conserved opening inventory. Parameters are illustrative and uncalibrated. See the [builder](record_aerospace.py) and [monthly results](out/aerospace_supply_scenarios.csv). |

Programme-specific approved sources, overlap in ownership, available throughput,
usable stocks, lead times and purchasing terms are required for an empirical
qualification-adjusted result or a validated recovery path. The checked-in
extract was checked for consistency and arithmetic. Full reproduction of the
bilateral trade aggregation needs the underlying CEPII files.
