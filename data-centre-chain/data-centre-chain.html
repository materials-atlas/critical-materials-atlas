What delays AI data-centre deployment?

An operating data centre needs compatible servers, electrical equipment, firm power and a completed facility. Which dependency sets the commissioning date varies by project.

Evidence review · 24 November 2026 · Claim register

Research question. When do hardware, electrical equipment, utility service or construction determine when computing capacity can go into operation?

Electricity use is growing, and the IEA identifies grid access and semiconductor supply as risks. The Atlas found that transformers gained relative to heavy machinery. Against motors, pumps and compressors, the uncertainty was wide enough to include no difference. Public project records show individual gates, including firm power approval and planning permission.

What must be ready for compute to operate

Computing hardware
Accelerators, memory and advanced packaging feed into integrated servers, storage and networks.
Utility service
The project needs a connection, any required network works, and deliverable firm power.
Electrical equipment
Transformers, switchgear, uninterruptible power and backup must be installed and tested.
Facility and acceptance
Permits, construction, cooling, hardware installation and integrated commissioning complete the phase.

Manufacturing concentration describes where capability sits. Scarcity concerns whether equipment can be delivered on time. A project constraint is a dependency that delays commissioning or limits usable IT capacity at a particular site. Cooling designs can require different amounts of water and equipment.

What the transformer evidence shows

The US Department of Energy reported that 36-month delivery quotes were common in 2024 for large power transformers, defined in the report as units rated at 100 MVA or more. Orders, delivery, installation and electrical acceptance are separate milestones. US DOE (2024), p. 2.

The Atlas compares liquid-dielectric transformer trade across three rating bands, split at 650 and 10,000 kVA. It compares 2023–2024 against 2012–2020. The transformer gaps against motors, pumps and compressors are smaller than those against heavy machinery, and both electrical-equipment intervals include zero. The filed pre-trend rule also failed, so the comparison is descriptive. September 2026 Atlas transformer study, §4.2.

Wider electrification may be pulling on shared manufacturing and installation capacity. The trade evidence alone leaves this hypothesis open. Testing it would require order books, capacity and delivery times by equipment class and buyer sector. The same Atlas study reports China’s share of world export value for grain-oriented electrical steel rising from 12% in 2019 to 32% in 2024. This tracks sourcing of traded steel, while manufacturing capacity and access to qualified grades need other evidence. Atlas AI build-out analysis.

The transformer study’s worldwide BACI data run through 2024. Its separate EU and US monthly evidence runs through July 2026. Those later records are importer data with different geographic coverage.

Documented project gates

Firm power in Memphis

On 11 February 2026, the Tennessee Valley Authority board authorized 150 MW of additional firm power for a Memphis data-centre load, subject to contractual, financial and operating requirements. The approval identifies a utility milestone. The minutes give no commissioning date. TVA board minutes, 11 February 2026, pp. 8–9.

Planning permission at Woodlands Park

In Iver, England, the inspector recorded that the proposed operator had secured a grid connection. The council had refused permission, and the Secretary of State granted it on appeal on 9 July 2025. This identifies a separate permission gate. The decision gives no operational date. Planning decision and inspector report, ¶5.3.

How the limiting activity can change

Illustrative schedule. The model starts one hypothetical phase in 2027-01, with 100 MW of planned IT load. At an assumed design power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.25, that requires 125 MW of facility power. The baseline assumes 125 MW of firm power. The project uses accelerator-heavy computing with uninterruptible and backup power, and the calculation treats the phase as available after integrated tests.

Each task starts when its prerequisites finish. Procurement, utility work and construction can run in parallel. Commissioning follows electrical acceptance, installation of computing hardware and installation of cooling. The model gives the same date when a faster delivery leaves another task controlling the schedule.

Across 27 combinations of assumed utility, transformer and memory durations, commissioning ranges from month 29 to month 42. These combinations have no assigned probabilities. The cases describe what happens under different assumptions and use no surveyed project durations.
