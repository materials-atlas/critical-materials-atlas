Method · honesty

What this is not — and what would change my mind

Every analysis is a set of choices, and choices can be wrong. This page states what the atlas is not, and lists each known limitation with the direction in which it biases the result. For the headline findings it also gives the concrete condition that would prove each one wrong.

What it is. A screening tool built entirely from public data: UN Comtrade / CEPII BACI trade, USGS & IEA production/refining shares, World Bank governance, and peer-reviewed satellite mine polygons. It maps where concentration sits across mine→refine→trade and flags where the trade picture hides the production picture. What it is not: it is not causal (it describes structure; it does not prove why), not a forecast (the 2025/26 figures are directional), not firm- or contract-level (it sees countries and HS codes, not companies or stockpiles), and not a substitute for proprietary mine-level intelligence. It shows where to look; it is not the last word.

Known limitations, by layer — with direction of bias

Tag shows which way each limitation pushes the headline: overstates makes concentration/risk look worse than reality, understates the reverse, uncertain either way. Where a limitation has already been mitigated, the green line says how.

Trade reconciliation is share-faithful, not level-faithful uncertain

BACI reconciles the two sides of every trade (exporter vs importer report) into one figure; the method is tuned to get shares right, not absolute dollar levels. Concentration metrics (HHI, top-partner share) are the right use; absolute values are indicative.

✓ The whole atlas is built on shares and ranks, not levels. The reconciliation engine is open and CI-tested against BACI.

The 2017 HS-vintage splice overstates

The long series joins HS-2002 codes (2002–2016) to HS-2017 codes (2017–2024). The origin-gap index steps at that join, so part of the “gap widened 15→18pp” is a splice artifact, not real drift.

✓ The step is caveated everywhere and marked on the Trends chart; the robustness page re-tests each trend separately on the two windows: 6 of 10 rising-concentration findings keep their sign in both halves.

Mine and refining shares are static, hand-entered approximations uncertain

The USGS/IEA production and refining shares are fixed values entered per material on mixed vintages. They do not move year-to-year, and one can go stale (a case-study audit caught a lithium mine share two years out of date).

✓ Caught and corrected via the case-study audit; a periodic USGS-refresh sweep is in the runbook. This is the atlas’s softest data and the first place to check a surprising number.

The network fragility figures are an upper bound overstates

The trade-network graphs are drawn from the top-6 partners per side for legibility. A truncated graph hides real alternative routes, so “removing China breaks X% of routes” is too high.

✓ Quantified on the truncation-sensitivity page: rebuilt fully uncapped, average fragility falls from 34% to 12%. China’s centrality, however, barely moves, and that finding survives.

Trend significance rests on 23 annual points overstates

Mann-Kendall on a short, serially-correlated annual series overstates significance. “10 materials are significantly concentrating” is an exploratory screen, not a confirmatory test.

✓ Re-run with an autocorrelation-robust correction on the robustness page: 8 of 10 survive. The rest are labelled exploratory.

The satellite footprint is all-commodity, not critical-material-specific uncertain

The Maus et al. mine polygons (Sentinel-2, ~2019) carry no reliable per-mineral label, so the country map shows total mining intensity (coal, iron, gold included), not critical-material footprint.

✓ Stated prominently on the satellite page; the curated flagship mines carry the material-specific view. Used only as a cross-check, never as a production measure. The limit has been quantified: stacking six independent public mine registers (Jasansky 2023 + USGS MRDS + OpenStreetMap + USGS critical-minerals PP1802 + Geoscience Australia + IPIS artisanal-mining sites) labels ~52% of the footprint and ties ~16% to a tracked critical material (up from 17% / 4% on Jasansky alone), and where two primary-commodity sources label the same mine they agree ~86% of the time. Adding the dedicated artisanal-mining dataset (IPIS, ~8,000 eastern-DRC sites) barely moved coverage: artisanal mining scarcely overlaps the ~2019 satellite footprint, so the imagery does not see it at all, labelled or not. The residual ~half is non-critical mines, register geographic gaps, and the by-product problem. More data triples coverage; it does not close the gap.

Implied unit values are noisy uncertain

Price = value ÷ tonnage from trade data mixes product grades and qualities. Read the price charts as trends and cross-country contrasts, not exact $/tonne.

Risk weights and disruption odds are assumptions uncertain

The fixed-weight index, the entropy weights (which reward dispersion, not importance), and the Monte-Carlo failure probabilities are stated assumptions, not estimated parameters. They are a panel of lenses, not a verdict.

The origin trace is a first-order upper bound overstates

Re-attributing refiner imports to the dominant mine is a single-step approximation that flags likely refiner-fronting. It is not proof of a disguised true origin.

Complexity is within 32 materials, not the economy uncertain

The RCA/relatedness layer measures specialization within this critical-material slice, not economy-wide economic complexity. The eigenvector ECI was dropped as degenerate on so few products.

What would change my mind

Concrete falsifiers for the headline claims. If these came back the other way, the finding is wrong.

“The origin gap is real, not a measurement quirk.” Wrong if, when 2025 BACI lands, the reconciled bilateral flows no longer reproduce the top-partner shares within the pre-registered thresholds. That test is locked in advance, pass or fail.
“Concentration is intensifying.” Wrong if the rising-HHI trends vanish under the autocorrelation-robust test (they do not: 8 of 10 survive) or flip sign across the vintage splice (most do not). A material whose trend fails both is not claimed.
“China is the trade-network broker.” Wrong if its centrality were an artifact of the top-6 cap. Uncapped, its throughput share moves only 21%→18% and it stays the top broker in 9/32 materials — so this holds.
“China’s dominance is partly processing, not tonnage.” Wrong if its export unit values sat at or below the rest of the world’s for the materials where its value-share exceeds its volume-share. They sit well above (tungsten +107%), so the processing-premium reading holds.
“The reconciliation engine reproduces.” Wrong if the CI run stops matching BACI on the committed fixture. It runs on every push and monthly; the badge shows the current result.

The atlas is open to checking. The code and data are public; the pre-registration is timestamped. If something here is wrong, it can be shown to be wrong.
