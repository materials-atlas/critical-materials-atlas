The origin gap, every material

Is the country that sells a material to the world the country that digs it up? For most materials it is not: a refinery, a smelter or a trading hub stands between the mine and the buyer.

The test is run on every material for which the atlas holds both world mine output and world customs trade, and it grows as the atlas does. Counted in tonnes instead of dollars the gap holds just as widely, so it is not an artifact of prices. Only for diamonds (rough, gem) is the top exporter a re-export hub that mines almost none of it. In the others the top exporter is a country that refines, smelts or processes the material.

How it is measured, and what it cannot show

One traded form per material. Each material is measured on its main traded form: refined or unwrought metal, or a ferro-alloy, for metals; the crude mineral for industrial minerals. A material traded at several stages would show a different exporter at each one. The customs code for every material added beyond the atlas’s own was fixed in codes.json before any result was computed.

Mine and trade come from different sources. Trade: CEPII BACI (HS 2017), 2024, by value. Mine output, 2024: for the atlas’s own materials its mined list (USGS, IEA); for the others the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries where held, otherwise World Mining Data, otherwise BGS World Mineral Statistics. For by-products such as cadmium, selenium and bismuth, “mine” output is the refinery production these sources report, and for sulphur it is mostly sulphur recovered from oil and gas.

Left out. tellurium: shares HS 280450 with boron; cannot be separated; uranium: 46 trade records in 2024; most uranium trade is confidential; fuels: crude oil, natural gas, steam coal, lignite: out of this pass by decision, may be added as their own section.
