Primary uses. superconducting magnet cryogenics; pressurizing/purging systems; controlled atmospheres.

⚗ A by-product, not a primary metal. Helium is not mined for its own sake — it is recovered as a by-product of natural gas. So its supply tracks the natural gas market, not its own price: a shortage can’t quickly pull more out of the ground, because miners dig for natural gas, not for this. That inelastic supply is a core, often-missed supply-risk driver.

The chain — from the ground to the buyer

A critical material passes through distinct stages, and the country that leads each stage is often different — that gap is what this atlas exists to show. Each layer comes from a different public source with its own vintage, labelled below; shares are % of the world total.

● Reserves — where it could come from

Reserves are the deposits known to exist and economically worth mining — the long-run ceiling on supply, distinct from what is actually produced today. Helium's reserves are moderately concentrated: 🇶🇦 Qatar holds the most (28%), ahead of 🇺🇸 United States (23%) and 🇩🇿 Algeria (22%). At today's extraction rate the known reserves would last roughly 216 years, so scarcity is not the binding constraint — access, capital and processing capacity are. Tellingly, the country sitting on the most ore (🇶🇦 Qatar) is not the one extracting it (🇺🇸 United States) — geology sets the ceiling, but capacity and policy decide who actually supplies the market. ● Mined — where it is dug up today

Mining is where ore leaves the ground — the stage most people equate with “the source”, though it is rarely where the supply risk actually concentrates. Output is concentrated: 🇺🇸 United States supplies 46%, with 🇶🇦 Qatar (39%) next. It out-produces reserve-richer 🇶🇦 Qatar while holding just 23% of reserves itself — supply power at this stage is built on installed capacity, not geology. USGS 2024 · 2023 production

● Refined / processed — where it becomes usable metal

Refining / processing converts ore into the metal or compound buyers actually purchase; it concentrates in fewer hands than mining and sits at the buyer's doorstep, which is why it is usually the true chokepoint. A country-level processing breakdown is not publicly reported for helium — only the leading refiner is known, which itself signals how opaque this stage is. leading refiner only — fuller breakdown not publicly reported
