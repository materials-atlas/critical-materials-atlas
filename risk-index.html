Risk & scenarios · one index, your weights

A supply-risk index with adjustable weights

Headline figures on this page: modelled

Every “critical materials risk ranking” is one concentration index with someone else’s weighting built in: on trade value or on tonnes, with or without governance, ignoring or crediting recycling. This page gives the reader the dial instead. Change the basis and the adjustments, and watch the ranking of 28 materials move. Where a material stays near the top under every setting, that is a robust dependency; where it swings, the “risk” came from the weighting.

Concentration is not vulnerability. The index measures how concentrated supply is and how exposed buyers are. It does not see stocks, substitutes, or how long a new supplier takes to qualify, because no public data covers them.

What this replaces, and why one page is better

The atlas used to carry this as several separate rankings (a value index, a tonnes index, a governance-weighted index, a recycling-adjusted index), each one an argument about which weighting is “right”. They were all correct and all incomplete, because the weighting is a choice, not a fact. One dial makes that visible and testable: a robust dependency (gallium, rare-earth magnets, tungsten) sits near the top however it is set; a fragile one moves. For an external check against an independent expert list, see the IEA cross-check. For whether concentration is measured correctly, see the per-commodity status map. This index takes concentration as given and shows only what it is multiplied by.

Ingredients (each material’s three HHIs, its top producer’s governance score, its recycling rate and substitutability) are assembled by build_risk_index.py from the atlas’s own risk_index.json. Governance from World Bank WGI; recycling/substitutability from USGS. Adjustments are transparent multipliers, shown below.

Weight eight criteria yourself

A second tool on the same question. Above, the index has three bases and three switches. Here each material carries eight criteria, and the weight on each is set with a slider. It was a separate page (the index builder) until 25 November 2026.

The weights are yours to set. Set the eight sliders however you like, and all 32 materials re-rank live, with the correlation to the published index updating as you go.
The eight criteria, and what you’re comparing against

Each material is scored on eight risk criteria, each normalised to 0–1 (higher = riskier): mine and refining concentration, trade concentration, China’s share, the origin gap, lack of recycling, hard-to-substitute, and producer governance risk. Your weights combine them into a 0–100 score. The published supply-risk index and the data-driven entropy-TOPSIS are two particular weightings; the correlations show how close your index lands to each.
