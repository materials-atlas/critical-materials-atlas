When does transformer procurement delay the grid?

Procurement is slow and trade in transformer core steel is concentrated. Which constraint delays a project depends on the equipment it needs and when the site is ready.

Evidence release · 24 November 2026

Manufacturing network Trade evidence Procurement Project timing Methods Sources

Executive summary

Large power transformers are slow to procure. The evidence points to pressure on manufacturing and on several qualified inputs, including core steel, bushings and tap changers. A global ranking of those constraints would require data on deliverable output and orders for comparable specifications. Customs evidence measures a different kind of exposure. In the EU, China’s share of imported transformers was much larger than its share on a value-based apparent-supply calculation. The transformer trade premium also depends on which equipment serves as the comparison group. These results support tracking supplier exposure, procurement pressure and project timing directly.

This paper considers large power transformers used in transmission, with European and US procurement evidence. The companion supplier-exposure analysis compares wider customs baskets. Their ratings and end uses are broader than the transmission equipment discussed here.

Several input routes converge at assembly

Public trade data distinguish some finished products and inputs. They leave the grade, specification, approved supplier, factory slot and input origin inside imported equipment unresolved. Copper and aluminium are both relevant to this network, with substitution depending on equipment design and qualification. A broad measure of metal production leaves their deliverability to a particular project unknown.

Core-steel trade is concentrated

Grain-oriented electrical steel (GOES) is the magnetic steel used in conventional transformer cores. From 2019 to 2024, China’s share of world GOES export value rose from 11.8% to 31.7%. It supplied 34.9% of recorded tonnes in 2024. The 2022–2024 pooled exporter HHI was 0.164 for GOES and 0.073 for liquid-dielectric transformers. HHI runs from zero to one in this comparison. The global calculation uses the same source, time window and value basis for both products. CEPII BACI, HS17 V202601.

Both value and tonnage show rising Chinese shares. Value and mass give different rankings for some exporters because prices vary and the basket combines grades and widths. The 2024 value HHI is 0.232 for sheet at least 600 mm wide and 0.082 for narrower strip. Customs records also combine original sale and re-export channels. Domestic sales, product mix and re-exports can push production concentration above or below export concentration.

Correction, 24 November 2026. The previous Grid Chain called its export HHI a lower bound on production concentration. The trade data support no directional bound. The 2022–2024 HHI is calculated after pooling annual export amounts. The published value uses pooled shares.

EU production changes the supplier exposure estimate

China supplied 34.2% of liquid-dielectric transformer imports and 9.1% on the value-based apparent-supply measure. For GOES the corresponding value shares were 51.9% and 24.2%. The independently pulled Eurostat data reproduce the rounded headline shares. Comparing the GOES baskets by mass gives 56.5% of imports and 26.3% of apparent supply, with some Prodcom tonnage rounded. China’s share of EU GOES import value rose from 9.2% in 2019 to 57.0% in 2025. These aggregates leave the geography of qualified substitutes and the origin of steel in imported transformers unresolved. Eurostat Prodcom and Comext.

Direct US exposure to the same exporting country is much smaller. In the September study’s 2024 US customs data, China accounted for 0.6% of GOES import value and 3.4% of liquid-dielectric transformer import value. Japan and South Korea supplied most US GOES imports. The national laboratory’s US study identifies heavy reliance on imported high-grade steel for large power transformers. A low share from China can coexist with substantial import reliance on other origins. The US customs numbers have no comparable apparent-supply denominator here. September trade study, section 4.7 and National Laboratory of the Rockies, Table 4.

The broad large-inverter basket covers several applications, including solar, storage and industrial uses. Moving the rounded EU sold-production input by 10% either way gives 65.5% to 82.5% on the value-based measure. The gross numerator includes imports that could later be re-exported. Public totals cannot trace their retention by origin. A high share for this basket gives no estimate of the share for transmission converters.

Eurostat flags the 2024 inverter sold-production value as rounded and publishes a €3 billion rounding base. Applying its documented rounding rule to production alone gives a lower share near 34.0%, with a potentially nonpositive supply denominator at the low production end. The data therefore give no useful upper share on this formula across the published rounding range. This is a separate diagnostic from the ±10% test. Eurostat DS-059358 quick guide, p. 2.

The trade comparison depends on the equipment selected

The larger premium appears against the heavy-machinery basket. The interval against motors, pumps and compressors includes zero. A separate transformer-specific shock remains unresolved. Re-estimating both from the original BACI flows reproduced the September estimates and intervals. The filed pre-trend rule also fails, so the estimates remain descriptive. Changes in the type and rating of equipment traded can move value per tonne independently of contract prices. The IEA survey and customs estimates can therefore report different changes for different baskets.

Long waits are documented across markets

Demand growth, existing orders and availability of qualified inputs can jointly lengthen lead times. ENTSO-E’s planned additions help define the European demand calendar and it reports limited visibility into manufacturing capacity. The US laboratory report identifies long component lead times from manufacturer interviews. Investment announcements report planned expansion. Deliverable capacity requires a dated picture of operational output, staffing, testing slots, key inputs, uncommitted orders, transport and eligible suppliers. The public benchmarks do not allocate the order delays to one stage.

A faster transformer helps when it changes the finish date

Earlier equipment delivery shortens the transformer-led schedule. It leaves the permit-led schedule unchanged, and has no effect when site work shares the last completion date. Faster delivery can still reduce risk from unexpected equipment delay. An actual project schedule would need dates for the permit, transport, construction, installation and network dependencies.

Agreement on delivery dates is the relevant test for a project. Evidence on tooling, skilled labor, acceptance testing, finished transformer inventory and qualified components is needed before attributing the industry’s waits to any single stage. For the EU exposure measure, sold production omits internal use, and the customs and factory-gate values differ. Inventories, changes in the product mix and re-exports matter. The share covers direct gross exposure and cannot identify final absorption or upstream materials embedded in equipment.
