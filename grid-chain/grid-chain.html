When does transformer procurement delay the grid?

Procurement is slow and trade in transformer core steel is concentrated. Which constraint delays a project depends on the equipment it needs and when the site is ready.

Evidence release · 25 November 2026 · first released 24 November 2026

Manufacturing network Trade evidence Procurement Order books and output Project timing Methods Sources

Executive summary

Large power transformers are slow to procure. The evidence points to pressure on manufacturing and on several qualified inputs, including core steel, bushings and tap changers. A global ranking of those constraints would require data on deliverable output and orders for comparable specifications. Customs evidence measures a different kind of exposure. In the EU, China’s share of imported transformers was much larger than its share on a value-based apparent-supply calculation. The transformer trade premium also depends on which equipment serves as the comparison group. These results support tracking supplier exposure, procurement pressure and project timing directly.

Added 25 November 2026. Official statistics and company accounts now quantify part of that pressure. The order backlog of the German branch that makes motors, generators and transformers covers 14.1 months of sales, against 5.9 at the end of 2019. The order books of four large makers equal 2.9 to 3.7 years of segment revenue. Where transformer output is counted in units, in Japan and the EU, the counts show no rise. Electrical steel is a different case: in the EU there was no shortage in total tonnage in 2025, with mills at 71% of capacity while imports rose, but the highest grades come from imports and the figures do not show whether those were tight.

This paper considers large power transformers used in transmission, with European and US procurement evidence. The companion paper on supplier exposure compares wider customs baskets. Their ratings and end uses are broader than the transmission equipment discussed here.

Several input routes converge at assembly

Public trade data distinguish some finished products and inputs. They leave the grade, specification, approved supplier, factory slot and input origin inside imported equipment unresolved. Copper and aluminium are both relevant to this network, with substitution depending on equipment design and qualification. A broad measure of metal production leaves their deliverability to a particular project unknown.

Core-steel trade is concentrated

Grain-oriented electrical steel (GOES) is the magnetic steel used in conventional transformer cores. From 2019 to 2024, China’s share of world GOES export value rose from 11.8% to 31.7%. It supplied 34.9% of recorded tonnes in 2024. The 2022–2024 pooled exporter HHI was 0.164 for GOES and 0.073 for liquid-dielectric transformers. HHI runs from zero to one in this comparison. The global calculation uses the same source, time window and value basis for both products. CEPII BACI, HS17 V202601.

Both value and tonnage show rising Chinese shares. Value and mass give different rankings for some exporters because prices vary and the basket combines grades and widths. The 2024 value HHI is 0.232 for sheet at least 600 mm wide and 0.082 for narrower strip. Customs records also combine original sale and re-export channels. Domestic sales, product mix and re-exports can push production concentration above or below export concentration.

Correction, 24 November 2026. The previous Grid Chain called its export HHI a lower bound on production concentration. The trade data support no directional bound. The 2022–2024 HHI is calculated after pooling annual export amounts. The published value uses pooled shares.

EU production changes the supplier exposure estimate

China supplied 34.2% of liquid-dielectric transformer imports and 9.1% on the value-based apparent-supply measure. For GOES the corresponding value shares were 51.9% and 24.2%. China’s share of EU GOES import value rose from 9.2% in 2019 to 57.0% in 2025. These customs baskets are wider than transmission equipment: the transformer lines run from small distribution units to large power transformers. The aggregates leave the geography of qualified substitutes and the origin of steel in imported transformers unresolved. Supplier exposure and scarcity are separate questions, and the companion paper treats the first. Eurostat Prodcom and Comext.

Direct US exposure to the same exporting country is much smaller. In the September study’s 2024 US customs data, China accounted for 0.6% of GOES import value and 3.4% of liquid-dielectric transformer import value. Japan and South Korea supplied most US GOES imports. The national laboratory’s US study identifies heavy reliance on imported high-grade steel for large power transformers. A low share from China can coexist with substantial import reliance on other origins. September trade study, section 4.7 and National Laboratory of the Rockies, Table 4.

The trade comparison depends on the equipment selected

The larger premium appears against the heavy-machinery basket. The interval against motors, pumps and compressors includes zero. A separate transformer-specific shock remains unresolved. Re-estimating both from the original BACI flows reproduced the September estimates and intervals. The filed pre-trend rule also fails, so the estimates remain descriptive. Changes in the type and rating of equipment traded can move value per tonne independently of contract prices. The IEA survey and customs estimates can therefore report different changes for different baskets.

Long waits are documented across markets

Demand growth, existing orders and availability of qualified inputs can jointly lengthen lead times. ENTSO-E’s planned additions help define the European demand calendar and it reports limited visibility into manufacturing capacity. The US laboratory report identifies long component lead times from manufacturer interviews. Investment announcements report planned expansion. Deliverable capacity requires a dated picture of operational output, staffing, testing slots, key inputs, uncommitted orders, transport and eligible suppliers. The public benchmarks do not allocate the order delays to one stage.

Order books and prices rose faster than output

Added 25 November 2026. No public statistic measures deliverable transformer capacity by rating. Several do measure order backlogs, prices and output for the industries and companies that make transformers. Only the US price index, the Japanese series and the EU production series are specific to transformers. The German and US backlog figures cover a wider group of electrical equipment.

In Germany the order backlog of the branch that makes motors, generators and transformers covered 14.1 months of sales in September 2026, against 5.9 months at the end of 2019. All manufacturing stood at 8.9 months. In the United States, unfilled orders for electrical equipment rose from 4.0 months of shipments at the end of 2019 to 6.9 months at the end of 2022 and were 5.8 months in September 2026, a rise of 45%. For all manufacturing industries that report unfilled orders the same measure went from 6.3 to 7.2 months, a rise of 14%. Electrical equipment has a shorter order book than that average, and it has lengthened more. The US producer price index for transformer manufacturing was 192 in October 2026 on a 2019 base of 100, against 146 for all manufacturing, a relative rise of 32%.

In Japan the value of transformer orders rose by 108% between fiscal 2020 and fiscal 2025. Units of 100 MVA and above produced averaged 48 a year in fiscal 2022 to 2025, against 72 a year in fiscal 2015 to 2018. Over the years with order data they went from 57 in fiscal 2020 to 46 in fiscal 2025, after a low of 37 in fiscal 2024. In the EU, the value of sold production of transformers above 10 MVA rose by 117% between 2021 and 2025 while the number of units was almost unchanged, at 3,792 and 3,733. Value per unit went from EUR 0.63 million to EUR 1.38 million. A unit count does not show size, so larger units can explain part of that rise, and so can higher prices. Neither series shows unit output rising. Large units take years to build, so recent orders would not yet appear in output.

The European Commission’s business survey asks manufacturers of electrical equipment what limits their production. The share naming a shortage of material or equipment was 4.2% at the start of 2015, peaked at 70.9% in the third quarter of 2022, and was 21.7% in the fourth quarter of 2026. In that quarter 16.3% named labour, 35.7% named demand and 38.1% named no limit. Capacity utilisation in the same industry was 80.0%, close to its level at the start of 2015. The survey covers all electrical equipment, and on it weak demand is still named more often than supply limits. European Commission business survey, electrical equipment.

One study has counted capacity plant by plant. It covers 324 factories in 51 countries making transformers above 10 MVA, and reports that demand grew by 23% from 2019 while nameplate capacity grew by less than 5%, with 216 GVA of additions identified for 2024 to 2027. It also reports that total nameplate capacity is above estimated demand, and offers two explanations: plants run below nameplate, or its model overestimates plant capacity. It is an industry analyst’s article in a trade journal. Christoffersen, Transformers Magazine, 2025.

Grain-oriented electrical steel. Union figures are from the questionnaire replies of the two Union producers. The share of consumption counts imports of steel laminations and cores as well as steel. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2133.

The European Commission adopted a provisional safeguard measure on imports of grain-oriented electrical steel, and of laminations and cores made from it, on 18 September 2026. It entered into force on 25 September 2026. Its preliminary investigation found that Union mills used 71% of their capacity in 2025, down from 85% in 2021, while imports of the steel grew 2.2 times and the Union producers’ share of consumption fell from 66% to 43%. China’s share of imports of the steel and of cores made from it rose from 20% to 53%. The Commission estimates existing world overcapacity at more than 842,000 tonnes. One of the two Union producers closed both its plants for the second half of December 2025, ran its French plant at half capacity from January 2026, and announced a further closure for June to September 2026. thyssenkrupp Steel, 26 March 2026.

These figures show no shortage of electrical steel in total tonnage in the EU in 2025: imports more than doubled from 2021 and Union mills ran at 71% of capacity. The same regulation is specific about grades. It records that “Union producers are predominantly selling GOES of lower grades, while imports consist to a large extent of higher grades.” It also found that Union producers could not make the highest quality needed for the most efficient transformers, and the producers agreed. So the idle capacity is in grades the mills make. The figures do not show whether the highest grades, which come from imports, were tight. The concentration of this trade, set out above, is an exposure to particular suppliers, most of all for those grades.

A faster transformer helps when it changes the finish date

Earlier equipment delivery shortens the transformer-led schedule. It leaves the permit-led schedule unchanged, and has no effect when site work shares the last completion date. Faster delivery can still reduce risk from unexpected equipment delay. An actual project schedule would need dates for the permit, transport, construction, installation and network dependencies.

Some specialised inputs also have long waits. Order backlogs at four large makers equal 2.9 to 3.7 years of segment revenue, and one maker reports filled production slots for one region.
Is qualified output short?For a specification, market and period, divide positive unmet demand by demand. For electrical steel in the EU there was no shortage in total tonnage in 2025: mills used 71% of capacity. The highest grades come from imports and are not measured separately. For transformers, unit counts in the EU (above 10 MVA) and Japan (100 MVA and above) show no rise while order values and prices rose. That is consistent with constrained output, and also with larger units, higher prices and the lag between order and delivery. Demand and available capacity by grade and rating remain unpublished.
Would another unit or earlier delivery speed up the project?Relax the candidate limit with other dependencies fixed, then recalculate output or completion.Demonstrated only in the stated model. It does not assign the frequency of transformer-led projects.

Agreement on delivery dates is the relevant test for a project. Evidence on tooling, skilled labor, acceptance testing, finished transformer inventory and qualified components is needed before attributing the industry’s waits to any single stage. For the EU exposure measure, sold production omits internal use, and the customs and factory-gate values differ. Inventories, changes in the product mix and re-exports matter. The share covers direct gross exposure and cannot identify final absorption or upstream materials embedded in equipment.
